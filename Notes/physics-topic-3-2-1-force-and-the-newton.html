Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton

Introduction

A force is generally regarded as a push or pull acting on one or more bodies. The difference in a force and an opposing force is called the resultant force. The newton is a derived SI unit, see Physics Topic 2.1.1 - Physical Quantities And Units for how it breaks down into base units. Forces and weight are vectors (Physics Topic 2.2.1 - Scalar And Vector Quantities), so the resultant of several forces is found with Physics Topic 2.2.2 - Scalar And Vector Calculations and Physics Topic 2.2.3 - Resolving Vectors.

Force, Mass, And Acceleration

When an object has no resultant force, its velocity will remain constant. (Newton's 1st Law) Acceleration is defined in Physics Topic 3.1.1 - Definitions In Kinematics.

Weight

The force which causes a falling object to accelerate is its weight, due to the relationship between force, mass, and acceleration. Weight in Newtons = Mass times Acceleration of free fall (g). The value of g used for weight is measured in Physics Topic 3.1.5 - Measurement Of G and used in Physics Topic 3.1.4 - Free Fall And Projectile Motion.

The Four Fundamental Forces Of Nature

  • The Gravitational Force - Every particle in the universe feels this. Proportional to product of the masses of the bodies, and inversely proportional to the square of the separation. Weakest of the 4, infinite range, always attractive.
  • The Electromagnetic Force - Holds atoms and molecules together, can be explained in terms of exchange of photons between charged particles and magnetic materials. Attractive or repulsive, infinite range.
  • The Weak Interaction - (The weak nuclear force) Process of radioactive decay, only acts over small range (\(10^{-18}m\)), weak in nature.
  • The Strong Interaction- (The strong nuclear force) Strongest of the four forces, holds subatomic particles together in the nucleus of atoms. Acts over small range (\(10^{-15}m\)). Quarks exchange particles called gluons that effectively hold protons and neutrons together.
    See Physics Topic 3.2.2 - Dynamics for tension, the normal contact force and friction, and Physics Topic 3.2.3 - Drag And Terminal Velocity for drag. The electromagnetic force acts between the charged particles in Physics Topic 4.1.2 - Electric Current And Charge.
