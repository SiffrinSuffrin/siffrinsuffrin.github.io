Physics Topic 2.1.3 - Systematic Errors And Random Errors

What Is An Error?

In science, an error is the difference between a computed/measured value and a true/theoretically correct value. See Physics Topic 1 - Physics Practical Endorsement for related terms such as anomalies, resolution and validity.

Random Errors

Random errors are errors that are literally completely random, because of the environment or human error while reading the results from an instrument. They are often wildly different from the other values. They reduce the precision of a set of results (Physics Topic 2.1.4 - Precision And Accuracy).

Systematic Errors

Systematic errors are errors with the measuring equipment, like it being broken, or misused, causing all results to be affected and incorrect. They affect the accuracy of every result (Physics Topic 2.1.4 - Precision And Accuracy).

Percentage Error

\(percentError = \frac{yourVal-actualVal}{actualVal} × 100\)
See Physics Topic 2.1.5 - Absolute And Percentage Uncertainties for how this compares to percentage uncertainty. Results hit by a random error are the major outliers that get discarded when drawing a line of best fit in Physics Topic 2.1.6 - Graphical Treatment Of Errors and Uncertainties.
