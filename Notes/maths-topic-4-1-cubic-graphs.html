Maths Topic 4.1 - Cubic Graphs

Introduction

A cubic function has the form \(ax^3+bx^2+cx+d\). It looks like this:

Treble repeated roots give "inflection points" on the x axis
See Maths Topic 2.4 - Quadratic Graphs for the quadratic case and Maths Topic 4.2 - Quartic Graphs for quartics. Roots can be found by factorising (Maths Topic 1.3 - Factorising), and Maths Topic 2.3 - Functions defines roots and functions.
Cubics can be intersected with other graphs (Maths Topic 4.4 - Points Of Intersection) and transformed (Maths Topic 4.5 - Translating Graphs, Maths Topic 4.6 - Stretching Graphs). A root that is hard to spot can be found with Maths Topic 7.3 - The Factor Theorem, then the cubic divided by it using Maths Topic 7.2 - Dividing Polynomials. Reciprocal graphs are in Maths Topic 4.3 - Reciprocal Graphs.
