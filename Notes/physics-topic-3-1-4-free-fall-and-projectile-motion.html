Physics Topic 3.1.4 - Free Fall And Projectile Motion

Why Do Objects Of Different Masses Fall At The Same Rate

A 10kg object is pulled down with 10x more force than a 1kg object, but is also 10x harder to move, so the rate of falling is the same.

Objects Thrown Upwards

Use the Physics Topic 3.1.3 - Constant Acceleration Equations (SUVAT) equations, with acceleration always being a constant \(-9.81m/s^2\).

Considering Vertical And Horizontal Motion Together

When an object is thrown at an angle, it has both a horizontal and vertical component. In the absence of air resistance, the horizontal velocity component of any projectile remains constant while it is accelerating downwards.
First off, resolve the starting velocity into the horizontal and vertical velocities (Physics Topic 2.2.3 - Resolving Vectors)
Then:

  • To find the highest point reached by the arc: \(v^2=u^2+2as\) where s is the max height, u is the vertical component, a is \(-9.81m/s^2\) and v is 0
  • To find the time to fall back down: \(s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2\) where t is the time taken to fall back down, s is 0, u is the vertical component, and a is \(-9.81m/s^2\)
  • To find the furthest point reached by the arc: \(d=vt\) where d is the distance travelled, v is the horizontal component, and t is the time taken to fall back down
    The time to fall back down comes from a quadratic in t, which can be solved with Maths Topic 2.1 - Solving Quadratic Equations.
