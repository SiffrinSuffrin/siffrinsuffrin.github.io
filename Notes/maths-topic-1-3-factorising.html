Maths Topic 1.3 - Factorising

Division

Factorising is the opposite of Maths Topic 1.2 - Expanding Brackets, where you take an equation (often a binomial), and write it as a product of it's factors.
Expanding a binomial turns it into a quadraratic expression, which is an expression in the form \(ax^2+bx+c\) where a != 0 and a, b, and c are real numbers.
An equation in the form \(x^2 - y^2 = (x + y)(x - y)\), and is known as the difference of two squares
Factorising is used to solve quadratic equations in Maths Topic 2.1 - Solving Quadratic Equations, and to find the roots of the graphs in Maths Topic 4.1 - Cubic Graphs.
Larger polynomials are factorised with Maths Topic 7.3 - The Factor Theorem and Maths Topic 7.2 - Dividing Polynomials, and cancelling common factors is used in Maths Topic 7.1 - Algebraic Fractions. The difference of two squares is what makes rationalising denominators work, see Maths Topic 1.6 - Rationalising Denominators, and identities are proved by factorising in Maths Topic 7.4 - Mathematical Proof.
