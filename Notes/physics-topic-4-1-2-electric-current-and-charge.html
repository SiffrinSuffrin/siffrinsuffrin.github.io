Physics Topic 4.1.2 - Electric Current And Charge

Introduction

Charge is a property of particles. Electrons have negative charge, and protons have positive. An atom can become charged if it gains electrons or loses them. In any metal, the flow is made of electrons. This is because of the long rows of ions within the metal, forming a grid that delocalised electrons within the metal are free to move within. This is why metals are good conductors. Some solutions (electrolytes) also conduct, but their charge carriers are positive and negative ions, but that's some chemistry nonsense so I don't give a crap. See Physics Topic 4.1.3 - Electron Drift Velocity for how the electrons move through the metal, and Physics Topic 4.1.1 - Electric Circuit Components for the circuit components they flow through.

Kirchhoff's First Law

Charge is always conserved, so "The sum of currents entering a point equals the sum of the currents leaving that point". The unit of charge is the Coulomb (1As (1 ampere second) = 1C), the quantity of charge that passes a fixed point in one second when a current of one ampere is flowing. See Physics Topic 4.3.1 - Kirchhoff's First And Second Laws for the full laws, and Physics Topic 2.1.1 - Physical Quantities And Units for how the coulomb is expressed in SI base units.

The elementary charge e

The charge on an electron, e, is \(−1.6 × 10^{−19} C\) (discard - for magnitude). Powers such as \(10^{-19}\) follow the rules in Maths Topic 1.4 - Negative And Fractional Indices. The charge on a proton is negative e, ie \(+1.6 × 10^{−19} C\).

Current Vs Charge

Current = Charge / Time, ie \(I = \frac{deltaQ}{deltaT}\)
