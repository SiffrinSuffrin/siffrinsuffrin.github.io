Physics Topic 2.2.1 - Scalar And Vector Quantities

Scalar quantities

A scalar quantity is a quantity with magnitude (size), and no direction, so they get bigger by "scaling".
A common example of a scalar quantity is speed
Scalars can be added, subtracted, multiplied and divided simply, as long as attention is paid to the units.

Vector quantities

A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction.
A common example of a vector quantity is velocity (speed + direction, for example travelling due north at 60mph). See Physics Topic 3.1.1 - Definitions In Kinematics for speed, velocity and displacement.
Vectors are slightly more difficult to add (see Physics Topic 2.2.2 - Scalar And Vector Calculations), and any vector can be resolved into perpendicular components (Physics Topic 2.2.3 - Resolving Vectors)
