Physics Topic 2.1.4 - Precision And Accuracy

Defining Accuracy And Precision

While precision and accuracy are often used interchangeably in everyday conversations, they actually mean different things.
A group of measured quantities is accurate if they are close to the true value.
A group of measured quantities is precise if they are very similar to each other.
Random errors reduce precision, and systematic errors reduce accuracy (Physics Topic 2.1.3 - Systematic Errors And Random Errors). Repeatability and reproducibility, which are both about precision, are covered in Physics Topic 1 - Physics Practical Endorsement.
See Physics Topic 2.1.5 - Absolute And Percentage Uncertainties for putting a number on uncertainty. Precise results have a small range, so the uncertainty taken from half the range is small and the error bars in Physics Topic 2.1.6 - Graphical Treatment Of Errors and Uncertainties are short.
