Physics Topic 3.2.3 - Drag And Terminal Velocity

Introduction

A fluid is either liquid or a gas. When any object moves through a fluid, it experiences drag. The effect of drag is always to oppose the motion currently taking place.

Friction And Air Resistance

Any objects in contact experience a frictional force. They do not depend on the velocity. However, the force of drag does depend on velocity. It can be shown that \(F_d = \frac{1}{2}pC_dAv^2\) where:

  • \(F_d\) is drag (N)
  • \(p\) is the density of the fluid (\(KgM^{-3}\))
  • \(A\) is the cross sectional area of the moving object (\(m^2\))
  • \(C_d\) is the coefficient of drag (no unit)
  • \(v\) is the velocity of the object (\(MS^{-1}\))
    Memorisation is not required. It can be simplified down to \(F_d = kv^2\), showing the force of drag is directly proportional to \(v^2\). Notice that drag is affected by the cross sectional area and the density. As air is less dense than water, the aerodynamic drag of a floating object is negligible.

Terminal Velocity And Drag

When an object falls through the air from a large height, eventually the drag on the object will become equal to its weight, as the velocity increases, so the velocity can no longer increase. This is called terminal velocity.
Drag is one of the forces in Physics Topic 3.2.2 - Dynamics, and at terminal velocity the resultant force (Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton) is zero, so the velocity stays constant. Free fall in Physics Topic 3.1.4 - Free Fall And Projectile Motion assumes no air resistance, and the equations in Physics Topic 3.1.3 - Constant Acceleration Equations stop applying once drag matters, because the acceleration is no longer constant. The velocity levelling off is seen on a velocity-time graph, see Physics Topic 3.1.2 - Graphs Of Motion. Another quantity that grows with the square of the speed is braking distance in Physics Topic 3.1.6 - Car Stopping Distances, and the units in the drag equation are in Physics Topic 2.1.1 - Physical Quantities And Units.
