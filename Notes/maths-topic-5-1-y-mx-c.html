Maths Topic 5.1 - Y = MX + C

Straight Line Equations

\(y = mx + c\) can represent any straight line equation, where m is the gradient of the line, and c is height at which the line intercepts the Y axis. It can also be written in "standard form", \(Ax + By + C = 0\), where A, B, and C are integers. More info at Maths Topic 5.2 - Equations Of Straight Lines

Finding Gradients

To find the gradient m of the line joining the points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\), the following equation can be used, \(m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}\)
See Maths Topic 5.3 - Parallel And Perpendicular Lines for the gradients of parallel and perpendicular lines and Maths Topic 5.5 - Modelling With Straight Lines for linear models. Gradients are also used in physics, for example velocity on a displacement-time graph (Physics Topic 3.1.2 - Graphs Of Motion) and in Physics Topic 2.1.6 - Graphical Treatment Of Errors and Uncertainties. Lines in this form are also the boundaries of the regions in Maths Topic 3.7 - Regions. The gradient of a curve keeps changing, so it is found with a tangent instead, see Maths Topic 12.1 - Gradients Of Curves.
