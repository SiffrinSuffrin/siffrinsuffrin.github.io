Physics Topic 2.1.5 - Absolute And Percentage Uncertainties

What Is Uncertainty?

Uncertainty is the amount a value will change slightly between identical measurement conditions due to a lack of sensitivity in the instrument we are using, or the skill of the person trying to repeat the same measurement.

Absolute Uncertainty

When taking any reading, your measurement will not be 100% accurate, as your measuring device does not have an infinite resolution. This is absolute uncertainty, and it is given as half the smallest division on the used measuring instrument. Always measure absolute uncertainty to no more than 1 significant figure. See the Resolution and Uncertainty sections of Physics Topic 1 - Physics Practical Endorsement.

Percentage Uncertainty

Percentage uncertainty is simply how much the absolute uncertainty affects your measurement, and is found (for a single value) through \(percentageUncertainty = (\frac{uncertainty}{measured}) × 100\).
For multiple values, take the mean and the range, halve the range, then use the halved range as uncertainty and the mean as the measured. If you are doing maths on more than one value, combine their uncertainties using the rules below.

The Rules For Combining Percentage Uncertainties

First, convert your absolute to %.
For a compound quantity of the form \(y = ab\) , the rule is: \(percentageUncertaintyY = percentageUncertaintyA + percentageUncertaintyB\)
For a compound quantity of the form \(y = \frac{a}{b}\) , the rule is: \(percentageUncertaintyY = percentageUncertaintyA + percentageUncertaintyB\)
For a compound quantity of the form \(y = a^n\) , the rule is: \(percentageUncertaintyY = percentageUncertaintyA × n\)
(for adding/subtracting, work with the raw absolutes)
For example, the cross sectional area of a wire depends on its diameter squared, so the percentage uncertainty in the diameter is doubled when finding resistivity in Physics Topic 4.2.4 - Resistivity.
See Physics Topic 2.1.6 - Graphical Treatment Of Errors and Uncertainties for uncertainty in the gradient and y-intercept of a graph.

Making Sure Your Answer Is Correct

To ensure your answer is accurate (Physics Topic 2.1.4 - Precision And Accuracy), % error (Physics Topic 2.1.3 - Systematic Errors And Random Errors) should be less than % uncertainty.
