Physics Topic 3.1.3 - Constant Acceleration Equations

The SUVAT Equations

Where S is the displacement, U is the intial velocity, V is the final velocity, A is the acceleration and T is the time interval:

  • \(v=u+at\)
  • \(s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t\)
  • \(s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2\)
  • \(v^2=u^2+2as\)

SUVAT on a graph

Assuming a straight line graph:

  • U is the y-intercept
  • V is the highest point the line reaches
  • T is the furthest point the line reaches
    See Physics Topic 3.1.2 - Graphs Of Motion for interpreting these graphs and Physics Topic 3.1.1 - Definitions In Kinematics for the definitions behind each variable. The equations are applied in Physics Topic 3.1.4 - Free Fall And Projectile Motion and Physics Topic 3.1.6 - Car Stopping Distances. The equation for s in terms of t is a quadratic in t, so Maths Topic 2.1 - Solving Quadratic Equations can be used to solve for time. The acceleration can come from a resultant force (Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton, Physics Topic 3.2.2 - Dynamics), and stops being constant when drag matters (Physics Topic 3.2.3 - Drag And Terminal Velocity).
