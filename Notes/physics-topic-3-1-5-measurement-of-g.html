Physics Topic 3.1.5 - Measurement Of G

Investigating Motion

Velocity and acceleration can be measured using motion sensors or light gates on a data logger. A light gate measures the time it takes for a card attached to a model or fall object to move between two points, while a motion sensor record displacement at a given interval. Both are more accurate than a stopwatch. See Physics Topic 2.1.5 - Absolute And Percentage Uncertainties for the uncertainty a stopwatch introduces, and Computer Science Topic 4 - Input Devices for sensors and how computers take readings from them.

Experimental Methods Used For Determining The Acceleration of Free Fall

  • Direct approaches, such as timing a falling ball and working out the acceleration due to gravity using the equations of motion (Physics Topic 3.1.3 - Constant Acceleration Equations, Physics Topic 3.1.4 - Free Fall And Projectile Motion).
  • Indirect approaches, such as measuring the time taken for a pendulum to complete a full swing, as it is dependent on g.
    Weight, which depends on g, is in Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton. A stopwatch relies on human reaction time, which is a source of error (Physics Topic 2.1.3 - Systematic Errors And Random Errors), and the ±0.2s stopwatch uncertainty is given in Physics Topic 1 - Physics Practical Endorsement.
