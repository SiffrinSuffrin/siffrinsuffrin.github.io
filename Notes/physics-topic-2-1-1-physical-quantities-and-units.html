Physics Topic 2.1.1 - Physical Quantities And Units

SI Units - International System Of Units

SI units are the basic units, used everywhere, to derive new ones. A few examples can be seen below.

Name Unit Symbol
Mass Kilograms kg
Length Metres m
Time Seconds s
Temperature Kelvin K
Current Amps A
Amount of substance Moles mol
Luminous intensity Candelas cd

Breaking Down SI Units

Any derived unit can be broken down into SI units, as they are formed from them.

Derived quantity Unit name Unit symbol Unit expressed in other SI units
force newton N kg × m × (s ^ -2)
pressure pascal Pa N × (m ^ -2)
energy/work done joule J N × m
power watt W J × (s ^ -1)
electric potential difference volt V J × (C ^ -1)
electric resistance ohm (omega symbol) V × (A ^ -1)
electric charge coulomb C A × s
frequency hertz Hz (s ^ -1)

You can use base units to confirm an equation representing physical quantities is right, as the units must be the same type. These units are used in later topics, such as the coulomb in Physics Topic 4.1.2 - Electric Current And Charge and the volt in Physics Topic 4.2.1 - Potential Difference And E.M.F.
The hertz and the giga prefix are used for clock speed in Computer Science Topic 2 - Processor Performance, and the ohm is covered in Physics Topic 4.2.2 - Resistance And Ohm's Law. Orders of magnitude use the same powers of 10, see Physics Topic 2.1.2 - Estimating Physical Quantities. The newton is used in Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton.
