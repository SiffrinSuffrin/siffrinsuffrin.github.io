Physics Topic 2.2.2 - Scalar And Vector Calculations

Scalar Arithmetic

Scalars can be added, subtracted, multiplied and divided simply, as long as attention is paid to the units.

Vectors In One Dimension

A vector quantity is represented by a line with a single arrowhead. The length is the magnitude, the arrow the direction.

Parallel Vectors

If the vectors are parallel, acting in the same direction, their magnitudes can be simply added together

Antiparallel Vectors

Anti parallel vectors travel on the same line, but opposite directions. Pick an arbitrary one, multiply the opposite vector's mangitude by -1, then add them

Perpendicular Vectors

Two vectors at right angle, apply pythagoras' theorem, \(A = \sqrt{B ^ 2 + C ^ 2}\), where A is the resultant vector, and B and C are the two initial vectors). If not at right angles, use protractor + ruler to construct a scale drawing of a vector traingle, then simply measure the resultant.

Adding Non-Perpendicular Vectors

Method 1 - Scale diagram
Draw out the vectors with a protractor and a ruler, where 1cm = X units of magnitude. Then, simply draw the resultant vector, and measure it.
Method 2 - Calculations Using Cosine and Sine Rules
Use either the cosine rule (\((a ^ 2) = (b ^ 2) + (c ^ 2) - (2 × b × c × cos(angle))\)) or sine rule (\(\frac{a}{sin(A)} = \frac{b}{sin(B)}\)).
Method 3 - Calculations Using Vector Resolution
Simply choose axis that would align a vector with them, then just use pythagoras' theorem.

Subtracting Vectors

Simply multiply the magnitude of one vector by -1, then do all your calculations as normal.
Finding the resultant of forces uses the same methods (Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton).
