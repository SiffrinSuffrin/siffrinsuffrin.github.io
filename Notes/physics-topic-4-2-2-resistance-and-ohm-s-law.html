Physics Topic 4.2.2 - Resistance And Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law

Georg Ohm discovered that increasing the number of cells in a battery that was connected to a length of wire produced an increase in the current, which is directly proportional given the temperature remains the same, which lead to him publishing Ohm's Law:
The current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference across it, providing that physical conditions remain constant.
You can use a circuit with a variable number of cells connected to a long thin wire to recreate the experiment in the modern period. Use an ammeter and voltmeter. Physics Topic 1 - Physics Practical Endorsement and Physics Topic 2.1.6 - Graphical Treatment Of Errors and Uncertainties cover the uncertainty in voltage and current readings.
This can be plotted on any linear graph with \(Y = MX+C\)
A straight line through the origin is the proportionality in Maths Topic 5.5 - Modelling With Straight Lines, using the line equation from Maths Topic 5.1 - Y = MX + C.

Resistance And The Ohm

Resistance is \(\frac{PD}{current}\), with the unit being volts per ampere, or an "ohm"
See Physics Topic 4.2.1 - Potential Difference And E.M.F for p.d. and Physics Topic 4.1.2 - Electric Current And Charge for current, the two quantities that resistance relates, and Physics Topic 4.1.1 - Electric Circuit Components for the components in a circuit. The ohm in SI base units is in Physics Topic 2.1.1 - Physical Quantities And Units. Physics Topic 4.3.1 - Kirchhoff's First And Second Laws uses resistance in the second law.

Factors Affecting Resistance

  • The material. In terms of resistance, insulators > semiconductors > metals
  • The length of the wire. Longer wire = Higher resistance
  • The cross-sectional area of the wire. Thicker = lower
  • The temperature. Resistance increases with temperature (unless it's a thermistor, in which case, the opposite occurs)
