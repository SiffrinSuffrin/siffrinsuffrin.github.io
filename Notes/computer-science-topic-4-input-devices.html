Computer Science Topic 4 - Input Devices

Common Examples

Stylus, mouse, keyboard, touch screen, trackpad. A keyboard sends character codes, see Computer Science Topic 29 - ASCII and Unicode.

Barcodes

Barcodes are used to identify items, to keep track of them. They come in two forms, the 1D linear barcode (classic striped barcode), and the 2D barcode (QR Code) which stores far more information.

Barcode Readers

  • Pen type - Photo diode next to light source, drag across bars at even speed. Variance in light intensity measures the data.
  • Laser type - Pen scanner, but it uses a laser beam. Commonly used in point of sale systems in supermarkets.
  • Camera based - Camera + image decoding in order to read data from a 1D or 2D barcode, commonly a 2D one. Often tolerant to damage and misprinting

Digital Cameras

A digital camera uses a CCD or CMOS senor made of tiny light sensors in a grid, with a colour filter over them. The data from each of these sensors is recorded into a file, allowing the image to be reproduced. CCDs are better than CMOS, but use far more power. It can be used in facial recognition technology too.

Radio Frequency Identification (RFID)

RFID chips are used to identify and track things, like barcodes, but work up to 300m, without line of sight. It is made of a small microchip, and a large coiled antenna. Active tags are larger, as their battery powers the tag, actively transmitting a signal for the reader to pick up. Passive tags are powered the EM energy from the reader's radio waves, once powered they send the information held back.

Sensors

Analogue or digital sensors give a computer physical information about the environment around it. (Often used in monitoring/control systems) See Computer Science Topic 5 - Output Devices for actuators, which are used alongside sensors, and Physics Topic 3.1.5 - Measurement Of G for light gates and motion sensors used to measure acceleration.

Sensor and image data end up as binary (Computer Science Topic 28 - Primitive Data Types, Binary and Hexadecimal) stored on the devices in Computer Science Topic 6 - Storage Devices. The operating system handles the data from devices like these, see Computer Science Topic 7 - Functions Of An Operating System for peripheral management. Embedded operating systems take sensor inputs, mobile operating systems add touch screen support, and every device needs a device driver, see Computer Science Topic 8 - Types Of Operating System. Thermistors and LDRs, whose resistance changes with temperature and light, are common sensors, see Physics Topic 4.2.3 - Resistance Of Circuit Components.
