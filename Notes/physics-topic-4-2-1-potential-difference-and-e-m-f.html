Physics Topic 4.2.1 - Potential Difference And E.M.F

Introduction

Electromotive force (e.m.f) - Energy transfer per unit of charge (ie \(\frac{transferred}{charge}\)). Other energy becoming electrical energy.
Potential Difference (p.d) - Work done per unit of charge (ie \(\frac{work}{charge}\)). Electrical energy becoming other energy. Difference in potential (ie, work that could possibly be done) at any two given points.
Both are measured in volts. A volt is one joule per coulomb (Physics Topic 2.1.1 - Physical Quantities And Units).

Measurement

P.D is measured by connecting a voltmeter in parallel to the electrical component. This is because a voltmeter gets the DIFFERENCE between the two points either side of the component, deriving its potential. Measuring the P.D of mains will read 230V, but across the component will be less. This lost difference is the energy transferred to heat the cord.

Energy Transferred To Each Charged Particle

When a potential difference is applied to a conductor, the electrons gain kinetic energy, the energy gained is \(\frac{1}{2}m_ev^2=eV\), where \(m_e\) is the mass of the electron, v is its speed, and \(eV\) is one electronvolt when V is 1V
The charge on an electron, e, is given in Physics Topic 4.1.2 - Electric Current And Charge. See Physics Topic 4.3.1 - Kirchhoff's First And Second Laws for how e.m.f. and p.d. relate around a closed loop.
Resistance links p.d. and current, see Physics Topic 4.2.2 - Resistance And Ohm's Law.
