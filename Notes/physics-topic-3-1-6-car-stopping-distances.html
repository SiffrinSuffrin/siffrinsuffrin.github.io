Physics Topic 3.1.6 - Car Stopping Distances

Introductions

Speed limits range from 20-70mph. People can be seriously injured even by comparatively slow moving vehicles.

Stopping Distances

Stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance
Thinking increases by 3m every 10mph.
Increasing the speed by a factor of two increases the braking distance by a factor of 4. This follows from the \(v^2=u^2+2as\) equation in Physics Topic 3.1.3 - Constant Acceleration Equations.

Thinking Distance Braking Distance
High speed High speed
Tiredness Poor road
Alcohol and drugs Poor tyres
Distractions Poor brakes
Age Car mass

A heavier car needs a bigger braking force for the same deceleration, see the link between force, mass and acceleration in Physics Topic 3.2.1 - Force And The Newton. Poor tyres and a poor road reduce the friction between the tyres and the road, see Physics Topic 3.2.2 - Dynamics.

Thinking Distance And Braking Distance

Thinking distance = reaction time * speed (distance = speed × time, see Physics Topic 3.1.1 - Definitions In Kinematics). Thinking distance is directly proportional to speed, which gives the straight line through the origin described in Maths Topic 5.5 - Modelling With Straight Lines.

Speed Thinking Dist Braking Dist Total Dist
20mph 6m 6m 12m
30mph 9m 14m 23m
40mph 12m 24m 36m
50mph 15m 38m 53m
60mph 18m 55m 73m
70mph 21m 75m 96m

Braking distance is proportional to speed squared, so a graph of braking distance against speed is half of the u-shaped parabola in Maths Topic 2.4 - Quadratic Graphs.
