Maths Topic 5.4 - Length and Area

Finding The Distance Between Two Points

To find the distance between two points \(A\) and \(B\), imagine a right angled triangke with hypotenuse \(AB\). The between the points \((x_1, y_1)\) and \((x_2, y_2)\) the equation \(d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1) ^ 2 + (y_2 - y_1) ^ 2}\) can be used to find the distance.
This is Pythagoras' theorem, which is also used to find the resultant of perpendicular vectors in Physics Topic 2.2.2 - Scalar And Vector Calculations. See Maths Topic 5.3 - Parallel And Perpendicular Lines for perpendicular lines.
The distance between points is the basis of the equation of a circle in Maths Topic 6.2 - Equation Of A Circle, and the midpoint of the same segment is in Maths Topic 6.1 - Midpoints And Perpendicular Bisectors. The cosine rule in Maths Topic 9.1 - The Cosine Rule extends Pythagoras' theorem to triangles without a right angle, and Maths Topic 9.3 - Areas Of Triangles covers the area of a triangle. The distance is often not a whole number, so it is left as a surd, see Maths Topic 1.5 - Surds.
