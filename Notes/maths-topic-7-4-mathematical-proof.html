Maths Topic 7.4 - Mathematical Proof

What Is A Proof?

A proof is a logical argument to show a statement (conjecture) is always true. A proof starts with previously established theorems, then works through the logical steps, and states what has been proven.

You can prove a statement is true by deduction, starting from known facts or definitions, then using logical steps to reach the desired conclusion.

In a proof:

  • State any info/assumptions
  • Show every step clearly
  • Make sure every step follows logically
  • Cover all cases
  • Write a statement of proof

To prove an identity:

  • Start with the expression on one side of the identity
  • Manipulate until it matches the other
  • Show working
    Proving an identity relies on algebraic manipulation, mostly Maths Topic 1.2 - Expanding Brackets and Maths Topic 1.3 - Factorising, with Maths Topic 7.1 - Algebraic Fractions for simplifying algebraic fractions along the way. Deriving the quadratic formula from Maths Topic 2.2 - Completing The Square (see Maths Topic 2.1 - Solving Quadratic Equations) is an example of proof by deduction. See Maths Topic 7.5 - Methods Of Proof for proof by exhaustion and by counter example.
